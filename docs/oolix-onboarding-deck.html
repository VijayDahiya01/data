Onboarding every party

What each side signs up for, what we need from them, and exactly how a Data Partner gets the Agent running, the ad code on their pages and their first creative slot live.

Buyer Data Partner Network sponsor Oolix
02Overview

Three parties, three different things to prove

Nobody onboards for the same reason, so nobody onboards the same way.

Buyer

Brings demand: a brand, a budget and a reason for advertising. Must prove it is a real business before it can submit anything to a Partner.

Data Partner

Brings audience and ad space. Must stand up software inside their own infrastructure, and prove it is running, before a single campaign can reach them.

Network sponsor

Brings curation: which Buyers meet which Partners. Holds no data and no veto over a Partner's decisions.

The asymmetry

A Buyer can be onboarded in an afternoon — it is paperwork and a brand. A Data Partner is an engineering project: they run our Agent, expose a read-only view of their own data to it, and integrate an ad slot. Plan for weeks, not hours.

03Why it is built this way

One boundary explains every requirement

Customer data never leaves the Data Partner. That single rule is why their onboarding involves software at all.

05Buyer

Buyer onboarding, step by step

  1. Create the organization

    Legal entity, trading name, contact. Type is BUYER, or BUYER_AND_PARTNER if they are also supplying audience.

  2. Pass business verification

    Until this clears, they can build campaigns but the Submit button stays disabled. The portal now says so explicitly rather than greying out silently.

  3. Register a brand

    Name, website and landing domain. Data Partners see the brand when deciding whether to accept — it is how they judge what will appear on their property.

  4. Invite the team

    BUYER_ADMIN creates and submits; BUYER_OPERATOR handles creatives. At least two people are needed in practice — whoever creates a request cannot approve it.

  5. Declare an advertising purpose

    A stable identifier such as travel_insurance_offer. Each Partner checks their customers' consent against this exact purpose, so it is not a label — it decides who can be reached.

  6. Optional: connect a CRM

    Issue an API key so lead outcomes flow back. Required only for outcome-based campaigns, where settlement depends on the Buyer confirming which leads qualified.

  7. Build an audience, then a campaign

    Describe who they want, see which Partners can evaluate it, request reach, then attach creative, budget and Partners and submit for review.

07Data Partner

Seven gates to READY_FOR_CAMPAIGNS

The system computes this ladder continuously. A Partner is not requestable until every step is complete — and it tells them which one is blocking.

Reading the blocker

Each incomplete state has its own name — POLICY_PENDING, AGENT_PENDING, CONNECTOR_PENDING, SEGMENTS_PENDING, PLACEMENTS_PENDING — so a Partner always knows which step they are on rather than just "not ready".

08Data Partner

What we need from a Data Partner

Commercial

  • Legal entity and verification documents
  • Advertising policy — which categories they will and will not carry
  • Payout terms — the model and unit price they will accept
  • Named approver to decide on campaigns

Technical

  • Somewhere to run the Agent — a container or VM in their infrastructure
  • A read-only database user scoped to a pre-computed table
  • An attribute mapping from their field names to ours
  • Ad slots defined on their pages, and the SDK integrated
Two separate admins are mandatory

Registering an Agent requires a PARTNER_SECURITY_ADMIN. The commercial PARTNER_ADMIN cannot do it, and the PARTNER_CAMPAIGN_APPROVER who accepts campaigns is a third role again. Ask for these names early — it is the most common reason technical onboarding stalls.

What we never ask for

No customer list. No database credentials for us. No copy of their data, hashed or otherwise. No third-party tag, and no identifier we can join across Partners. If someone offers a customer export, the integration is being done wrong.

09Data Partner · Agent

The Agent: what it is and where it lives

A single Go binary the Partner runs themselves. It is the only component that sees a customer, and it never belongs to us operationally.

RequirementDetail
RuntimeOne static binary, or the container image we ship. No runtime dependencies to install
PlacementInside their network, reachable privately by their own backend. Never exposed to the public internet
Outbound accessHTTPS to the Oolix API only. No inbound connection from us, ever
Database accessA read-only user against one pre-computed attribute table, plus membership and consent lookups
Local storageA writable path for its private key and, in single-replica mode, its frequency counters
ScalingSingle replica uses in-process state. More than one replica requires Redis, or caps would be counted per replica and silently multiply
Latency budgetAnswers in under 100 ms; its database queries are capped tighter still
Their operational control

They can stop it at any moment, and revocation from our side takes effect immediately rather than when a credential expires. Nothing about the Agent is a black box they cannot switch off.

How the Agent gets its identity

The credential that matters is created inside the Partner and never transmitted. We only ever hold a public key.

11Data Partner · Agent

The mapping: their field names, our vocabulary

This one block of their config is why the whole model works without us seeing their schema. 

# The Partner writes this. Nothing we send can add to it.
connector:
  type: postgres_view
  dsn:  postgres://oolix_agent_ro:•••@db.internal/customers  # read-only
  query_timeout: 50ms

  audience:
    attribute_table: oolix_audience_attributes   # pre-computed, not live joins
    mapping_version: 8
    mappings:
      age:                   date_part('year', age(dob))
      purchase_category:     product_class
      payment_method:        pay_mode
      purchase_recency_days: date_part('day', now() - last_order_at)

What this means

We ask about payment_method; they store pay_mode. We ask about age; they store a date of birth. The translation lives in their file, on their machine.

What it controls

An attribute absent from this map is one they cannot be asked about. A campaign requiring it simply will not match them — which is the honest answer, not a partial one.

Insist on a pre-computed table

The attribute source must be a restricted, pre-built table — never a live join across their production schema. At scale, the difference is a query that returns in milliseconds versus one that scans hundreds of millions of rows on a page render.

12Data Partner · Integration

The ad code on their pages

Two pieces: a small SDK in the page, and one endpoint on their own backend. Nothing calls us directly from a browser.

The integration, in full

Drop the SDK into the slot, and add one backend route that resolves the signed-in customer and forwards the request to the Agent. That is the entire engineering surface on their pages.

14Data Partner · Placements

What fills the placeholder, and what happens when nothing does

An ad must never break their page

If the Agent is slow, unreachable or refuses, the SDK falls back and the page carries on. An advertising failure is not allowed to affect a booking, a checkout or a login — so the fallback path is the normal path, not an error path.

15Data Partner

Publishing what they can be asked about

A Partner with nothing published is invisible. There are two ways to be discoverable, and they are not equivalent.

Audience capabilities

They declare which attributes they can answer questions about — age, purchase category, payment method — and which operators apply. Buyers then describe an audience in our shared vocabulary and we work out who can evaluate it.

This is the primary path. It requires no audience to be built in advance.

Prebuilt segments

They publish an audience they already maintain — its name, description and a reach range. Buyers target it directly.

Still supported for Partners with an existing setup, but no longer offered to Buyers as an alternative way to build a campaign.

Reach is always a range

A published audience never carries an exact count, and reach estimates come back as bands. Anything below the minimum cohort reports only that it is too small — because "fewer than a thousand" is itself a disclosure.

16Network sponsor

Network sponsor onboarding

A sponsor curates who meets whom. They hold no customer data and cannot overrule anybody.

  1. Create the organization

    Type NETWORK_SPONSOR, with a NETWORK_ADMIN named.

  2. Pass business verification

    The same gate as everyone else.

  3. Choose an admission mode

    Private — invite only. Curated — applications reviewed. Marketplace — open. This decides how Buyers and Partners join.

  4. Admit members

    Buyers and Data Partners are invited and accepted into the network. Membership is what makes private supply visible to a Buyer at all.

  5. Set network policy

    The rules governing who may join and what may be transacted within it.

The limit that defines the role

A sponsor can curate supply but can never override a Data Partner's approval decision. There is no endpoint that would allow it. They see aggregate performance across the network, never a member's customer data.

19First campaign

What the first live campaign looks like

Once all three are onboarded, this is the sequence — and where each party acts.

  1. Buyer describes an audience

    In our shared vocabulary, not the Partner's field names. Marks each condition required or optional.

  2. Oolix matches Partners

    On published capability metadata only. A Partner missing a required attribute is incompatible and cannot be selected.

  3. Each Partner's Agent estimates reach

    Computed inside them, returned as a band. We never see the number behind it.

  4. Buyer builds the campaign

    Creative, budget per Partner, placement, flight dates — then submits.

  5. The Partner decides

    They see the full rule set, the creative, the terms and their own reach estimate. They can accept, accept less than asked, request changes, or refuse.

  6. The Agent builds the audience locally

    It verifies the signed instruction, compiles the approved rules against their data, and materializes the matching customers on their side.

  7. Ads serve

    Within the cap, the flight window and the consent each customer gave for that stated purpose.

  8. Outcomes flow back

    Hashed tokens attribute clicks; the Buyer's CRM confirms which leads qualified; settlement is reproducible from the record.

The Partner keeps control throughout

They can revoke at any point, and it takes effect immediately. Nothing runs on their property that they did not individually approve, and no approval can be granted on their behalf.
